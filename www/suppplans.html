SUPPLEMENTARY PLANS AND REPORTS
The following formats of these plans and reports and guidelines in preparing them have been derived from the Philippines ICS Training Materials developed by the Office of Civil Defense.
Contingency Plan

Contingency plans are prepared by the Planning Section with input from individual technical specialists and the Operations Section. The Operations Section Chief is responsible for pulling together the tactical pieces of contingency planning and giving them to the Planning Section. The Planning Section in turn is responsible for pulling the all relevant pieces together and making them organized and useable. Contingency plans provide direction on incident-related activities such as serious accidents / injuries, structural protection, evacuation procedures, hazardous materials procedures, and risk assessment.

Contingency Planning Considerations:

  1. Consult agencies and local cooperators
    Don’t forget to get other agencies' input and buy-in on responsibilities and actions, such as law enforcement, local government units, local agencies, utility companies and other expertise available. Even if an agency doesn’t have a contingency plan, you would still want to consult them in the development of a plan because it creates buy-in. Many agencies have pre-planned for these events. The IMT can utilize them as a foundation for the plan, and in some cases, support these plans.
  2. Review existing plans and Mutual Aid Agreements
    Local government units and agencies may have formulate their own Standard Operating Procedures, disaster response plans, and DRRMC Contingency Plans. The plans that the IMT will develop should take into consideration the activation and use of these plans.
  3. Conduct Threat / Risk Analysis
    Utilize Technical Specialists and local experts if available. Map potentially threatened areas based on your survey and the anticipated weather. Meteorologists and other experts may develop potential spread models or scenarios. Weather specialists can give you an idea of where the threat may occur, and may provide you with standard deviations. Determine the probability of the event occurring based on the intelligence gathered.

    In some instances, trigger points may be set. If the incident reaches a certain point, then the probability is great enough that action must be initiated for safe and effective operations to occur. Using these trigger points, the IMT can determine tjeamount of resources needed and time needed to obtain them, get them in position, and determine the rate of approach.

    The IMT may need to pay particular attention to areas of safe refuge, safety zones, triage of values at risk, and availability of resources.
  4. Develop Resource Needs Based on Likely Scenarios
    Review the amount of available resources required to protect the identified values at risk. Plan for additional needs such as: staging areas, water supply, and relief personnel. Use the ICS Form 215 to break into manageable divisions or groups.  If multiple groups or divisions are required, consider assigning a Branch Director.

    Order overhead for advanced planning of divisions or groups. Let them conduct triage and surveys. Resolve any identified problems. Utilize them to develop, fine tune, or revise the contingency plan.
  5. Obtain IC Approval as to Form and Content
    By its existence, a contingency plan can become very political and potentially controversial. Make sure the whole IMT is included in the development and dissemination, as the successful implementation of the Contingency Plan is dependent on the support of the entire team.

Suggested Format:

  1. Problem Statement - Covers the overall problem faced.  Is an introduction and description of what may / is expected to occur.  Orients the reader.
  2. Objectives of the Plan - Covers the overall plan.  Includes what/how/why/when you intend to accomplish.  Orients the reader and gives them the big picture of the strategy.
  3. Pre-event Action - Lists available, permissible steps that can be taken during the preparation phase.  Certain areas may have restrictions, legal or otherwise that may guide your actions.
  4. Tactics - Tactical options should be well known by participants; however, some areas may have specifics that need to be covered.  Local experts, pre-positioned overhead, and other agencies may have helpful material.
  5. Other Attachments
    • Maps
    • Triage Guides
    • Search Marking System
    • Safe Refuge Areas
    • Escape Routes
    • Evacuation Routes
Demobilization Plan

The demobilization plan creates a framework that supports the incident’s safe release of resources according to established timeframes. It supports the accurate transfer of information and requests for demobilization to expanded dispatch. Information and scheduling is shared with all resources assigned to the incident. The plan outlines the methods for accurate tracking of demobilizing resources.

Suggested Format:

  1. Signature Cover Sheet
    The demobilization plan must be approved and signed in the cover sheet by all Command and General Staff, the Expanded dispatch coordinator. Copies of the plan will be distributed to the signatories and displayed in appropriate locations.
  2. General
    This section identifies which expanded dispatch or other administrative unit will coordinate with the incident for the release of all resources. This section states the IC’s expectations of a safe and efficient release of resources, as well as safety and wellness considerations such as condition of resources (rested, showered), driving and travel limitations, and special or unusual safety conditions.
  3. Release Priorities
    This section contains the following:
    • Priority list for releasing resources
    • Release lead time guidelines for all resource types as required by expanded dispatch or the GACC.
    • Incident policy regarding performance evaluations and training documentation as determined by IC.
    • Best value release issues should be discussed with FSC for clarification.
    Priorities are based on several influencing factors such as:
    • Strategic needs of the incident
    • Need to release local resources back to home unit
    • Meeting contractual obligations for contract resources
    • Meeting cost containment requirements, including travel (such as five crews on one flight).
    Priority list may be subject to change based on resource demands on and off the incident.
  4. Responsibilities
    This section outlines the responsibilities of all parties involved in the implementation of the plan.The DMOB coordinates with section chiefs to determine specific demobilization processes necessary to meet each section ’s needs.

    Examples of responsibilities that may be included in this section are:
    1. Demobilization Unit
      • Compile tentative and actual release list.
      • Notify personnel of tentative and actual releases.
      • Ensure Demobilization Checkout (ICS-221) is completed.
      • Monitor the process and make adjustments.
      • Make flight requests to expanded dispatch and transmit transportation needs to ground support (lowboys for equipment, busses, travel to airport).
      • Communicate actual departure times and remain-over-night (RON) information.  
      • Follow any definitive priorities set by the hosting agency. Any negotiations should include the PSC.
    2. Logistics Section
      • Facilities Unit checks crew sleeping areas.
      • Supply Unit accounts for non-expendable property.
      • Ground Support Unit inspects vehicles, noxious weed control, and arranges for airport or other transportation. 
      • Communications Unit accounts for radios.
      • Food Unit provides meals if necessary and adjusts meal needs for caterer or other food provider.
    3. Finance / Administration Section
      • Completes all incident personnel finance records, such as crew time reports.
      • Compensation claims
      • Contract issues 
    4. Expanded dispatch
      • Ensures travel notification requirements such as ETA are met.
      • Arranges commercial transportation and associated lodging if necessary.
      • Coordinates set up and use of staging areas.
      • Arranges for rest and recuperation facilities.
    5. All Section Chief
      • Submit tentative demobilization requests to the DMOB.
  5. Release Procedures
    This section is a detailed outline of the procedure to be followed by the incident for properly releasing resources. Minimal procedures include:
    1. DMOB develops tentative release dates and time.
    2. DMOB gives resource list to section chiefs who will identify tentative release dates and times. The section chiefs return list to DMOB.
    3. DMOB consolidates list of tentative releases. This list is complete with air requests as required, and forwards to expanded dispatch for approval.
    4. Expanded dispatch provides reassignment information; air travel itineraries and approval to release resources not reassigned.
    5. DMOB develops an abbreviated release list and displays it throughout ICP (request number, name, date/time).
    6. DMOB notifies ground support (airport transportation) of demobilizing resource needs.
    7. DMOB notifies demobilizing resource of schedule and completes Demobilization Checkout (ICS-221) process when appropriate.
    8. DMOB sends actual release list (include ATD from incident/ETA at home) to expanded dispatch and relevant sections.
    9. DMOB ensures the resource status system is updated.
    10. DMOB ensures documentation is sent to DOCL.
  6. Travel Information
    This section describes the type of information that needs to be provided to the demobilizing resource to ensure efficient travel. This may include:
    1. Incident and expanded dispatch contact phone numbers, radio frequencies, etc. Calling cards if appropriate.
    2. Travel agent phone numbers.
    3. Maps and possible travel routes.
    4. Preparation of equipment for travel (purging fuels cans, weed wash station, etc.).
    5. Travel information/plans for reassignment are arranged for by DMOB through expanded dispatch.
  7. Emergency Demobilization Procedures
    This section outlines the process for managing emergency demobilizations, including medical, personal, and disciplinary.
    1. Identifies roles and responsibilities of specific positions (human resources, ground support, security, etc.) to support emergency demobilization with 24 hour availability.
    2. Provisions should be made for 24 hour availability.
Transition Plan

The transition plan outlines the process for IMTs to overlap and transition with their counterparts. It is specially required for complex incidents. Responsible Official and all ICs approve transition plan. The transition plan also provides a brief overview of the most important issues.

Suggested Format:

  1. Cover Sheet - Approved by the all outgoing and incoming Incident Commanders, and optionally by the Responsible Official.
  2. Introduction - Contains a brief description of the incident, including the objectives and the actions currently being undertaken.
  3. Transition Schedule - This section highlights schedule of activities to be undertaken by the outgoing and incoming IMTs. This includes the briefing, meetings, overlaps, and shadowing schedules.
  4. Agency Administrator / Responsible Official Briefing - This contains information on who will conduct the Agency Administrator / Responsible Official Briefing, as well as the planned agenda and timeframe.
  5. IC to IC Briefing - Contains information on the conduct of the Transfer of Command procedures, both from outgoing IC to incoming IC, and outgoing IMT to incoming IMT.
  6. Command Structure - Contains information on the current organizational structure, as well as planned changes to the structure to be implemented.
  7. Management Objectives / Incident Objectives - This section discusses the method of acquiring the management objectives, as well as the incident objectives as set by the outgoing IC. It also contains the status of the accomplishment of the said objectives.
  8. Supporting Organizations - Contains a list of agencies and organization supporting the management of the incident. It also outlines the various roles of these support organizations and agencies.
  9. Critical Issues - This section highlights the issues and concerns identified by the Command and General Staff that may affect the management of the incident if not properly addressed.
